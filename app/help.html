How to use

  1. On the Convert tab, choose or drop your disc files. Pick the .cue (or .gdi, or CloneCD .ccd) together with all of its .bin/track files (for CloneCD, the .img).
  2. Each game appears as a card. The right chdman command is chosen automatically; open Options to change compression, hunk size or the output name.
  3. Press Create CHD on a card, or Start all at the bottom. Jobs run one after another.
  4. When a job finishes, press Download. On desktop Chrome and Edge you can also Save all to folder.
  5. On iPhone and iPad the button is called Save to Files and opens the share sheet, which works even inside apps that can't download. Results over 512 MB, such as most DVD games, are downloaded instead, since the share sheet loads the whole file into memory. Safari puts them in Files → Downloads; apps like Sitecase usually can't, so for big games use Discpress in Safari at powerbeef.github.io/discpress/.

Add .chd files to extract them back to .cue/.bin, .gdi, .iso or a raw image, to verify their checksums, or to see their details. A CD comes back as a Redump dump by default: the same cue sheet and one .bin per track, so the files match Redump's checksums. With Settings → Keep the cue sheet in CD CHDs, new CHDs also keep the original cue sheet, so what CHDs don't otherwise hold (CATALOG, FLAGS, ISRC, extra indexes, CD-i track types) comes back too.

A lone .bin without its .cue still works for single-track data discs (a cue is generated), but for discs with music tracks always add the original .cue.

ECM images (.bin.ecm, packed with the ecm tools) can take the place of their .bin: add them with the .cue. They are unpacked as they are read, and a damaged one is reported.

A .zip works like the files inside it: add the zip of a game (or several), and its cue sheet finds its tracks inside. Files stored in the zip are read in place; compressed ones are unpacked first into the browser's private storage and checked against the zip's checksum. .7z and .rar can't be read: unpack them, or zip them again.

A PlayStation EBOOT.PBP (made with popstation for a PSP or PS Vita) converts to a CD CHD: each disc in it gets its own card, named after the game in the file. PlayStation Store downloads are encrypted and can't be read.

DiscJuggler .cdi, Alcohol 120% .mds/.mdf and .isz images get a card that says why they can't be converted and what to do instead. Emulators that play .cdi images (Flycast, Redream) load them directly, so keep those as they are.

The type and hunk size are set from the console once the game is identified. Compression stays chdman's default, which every emulator reads; the page warns when the Zstd preset isn't readable by a console's emulators (Saturn, 3DO, Sega CD, PC Engine CD, Amiga). GameCube, Wii, Xbox and PS3 emulators don't load CHDs at all, and the page says so. It also says when a common emulator can't load a CHD of the disc: PCSX2 plays only the first track of a PS2 CD with music tracks, and Flycast 2.7 and earlier can't load CD-based Dreamcast discs whose tracks have pregaps. For MiSTer FPGA, choose the For MiSTer FPGA compression preset (Zstd and FLAC with small hunks, which MiSTer reads fast enough).

Add a PAL PlayStation game's .sbi file (LibCrypt protection data) with its .cue and .bin: it is saved next to the CHD, under the CHD's name, where emulators look for it. A CloneCD dump's .sub holds the same data: for a PlayStation game, the page reads it and saves the .sbi for you. When two or more discs of a game are converted, the first disc's card offers an .m3u playlist: keep it with the CHDs and open it, so the emulator can swap discs.

Game identification and renaming

Every disc you add is examined to find its console (PlayStation 1/2/PSP, Saturn, Sega CD, Dreamcast, PC Engine CD, PC-FX, Neo Geo CD, 3DO, CD-i, Amiga CD32/CDTV, NAOMI, GameCube, Wii, Xbox, PS3 and more) and its serial number, read from the disc's own boot files and headers.

The game is then looked up in a built-in copy of the Redump database (about 42,000 discs, via libretro-database, /*DBVER*/). When a track's size and CRC-32 checksum match, the dump is an exact, verified copy; otherwise the serial number is used. Results are named after the official title, for example Metal Gear Solid (USA) (Disc 1) (Rev 1).chd. You can turn this off in Settings or type your own name in Options.

For existing .chd files, choose Rename to save a copy with the official name. Verify checks the CHD's own checksums, then compares it with Redump without extracting it: the file the built-in database lists for the disc (its ISO, its only .bin, or its main data track) must have Redump's size and CRC-32.

To check every track, add Redump's or No-Intro's DAT files for your consoles (the .dat or .xml, or its .zip), or add them in Settings → Your DAT files. They are kept in the browser. Each disc is then checked against them before converting, and each CHD after Verify: when every track matches a game in a DAT, the card says it is verified and the result is named after that game.

Good to know

Privacy

Nothing is uploaded. The real chdman from MAME runs inside your browser as WebAssembly; files are read directly from your device.

Big files

Results are written to the browser's private storage on disk (not to memory), so multi-gigabyte DVD images work if your device has the free space. Some browsers give no such storage to a page opened as a file (Chrome and Edge among them): the page then keeps results in memory and says so at the top, and after a reload it lists the results that were lost. For large DVD images there, choose a folder, or use Discpress online. Download results when they are ready; the storage is cleared when you use Free temporary storage or reopen the page, except results not saved yet, which the page lists under From your last visit. On desktop Chrome and Edge you can instead have results written straight into a folder of your choice (Settings → Where to keep results).

Speed

Creating a CHD is CPU-heavy. This page spreads compression over several CPU threads (see Settings). Expect a few minutes for a CD on a computer and longer on a phone. Nearly as small, faster in Options tries each track with only the codec that suits it: about 1.7 times as fast for CDs and 1.5 for DVDs, with files at most 0.3% bigger. Faster to create trades more size for more speed.

On phones and tablets

The layout adapts to your screen, text size, notch, rotation and keyboard. In a normal browser the whole page scrolls; inside apps that preview files (and when installed on the home screen) the header stays fixed and only the content scrolls. If scrolling ever feels wrong, change it in Settings → Scrolling; Settings → Screen info shows what your device reports.

Keep this page open and in the foreground while it works; the screen is kept awake automatically. If the browser suspends the tab, the job may stop and need to be restarted. On iPhone and iPad, iOS pauses a page within seconds of locking the phone or switching apps, and may reload it to free memory. A result that was finished but not yet saved is then kept, and listed under From your last visit the next time the page opens.

Two ways to run it. Discpress is one HTML file you can download and keep, or use online at powerbeef.github.io/discpress/ with nothing to download. It's the same file, published with each release, and it works on your device in both cases: the page may not connect anywhere. Once opened, the online version works offline too.

iPhone and iPad: use Discpress online, in Safari. Safari can't open a downloaded HTML file, and apps that can, such as Sitecase, usually can't save results over 512 MB. In Safari, results of any size download to Files → Downloads. Share → Add to Home Screen keeps it one tap away; if a big download doesn't start from there, open the page in Safari itself.

Compatibility of the results

The CHDs are standard CHD v5 files made by chdman from the official MAME 0.289 release, with the same checksums as desktop chdman 0.289 and the very same bytes as chdman 0.289 on Linux (unless you choose Nearly as small, faster or Faster to create, or keep cue sheets in CD CHDs, which change the bytes but not the checksums), so they work in MAME, RetroArch, DuckStation, PCSX2, PPSSPP, Flycast and other emulators. The Zstandard option needs a recent emulator build.

Everything else

The Advanced tab exposes every chdman command and option: copy, createraw, parent CHDs, hard disk templates, addmeta, delmeta, dumpmeta, listtemplates and more.

About and licenses

Discpress contains chdman from MAME 0.289 (the official release, tag mame0289), compiled to WebAssembly with Emscripten. Its source, with only the parts of MAME that chdman uses, is kept in the Discpress repository (engine/). The changes:

  • a file-system bridge for reading your files and writing results;
  • helper workers that compress, and decompress for extracting and verifying, on several cores;
  • faster codec trials, hashing and CD error-correction codes that give the same bytes;
  • Redump's layout for extracted CDs (extractcd --redump), and an option to keep a disc's cue sheet in its CHD for that (createcd --keepcue);
  • a codec plan that tries each hunk only with the codecs meant for what it holds (--codecplan), and deflate by libdeflate (--libdeflate), for the faster presets;
  • reading CSO/ZSO compressed ISOs, ECM images, PS1 PBP files and zip archives as the images they hold;
  • fixes for chdman 0.289's crashes and silent failures on bad input;
  • the same floating-point math for the FLAC encoder in every build, so CD audio comes out byte for byte as with chdman on Linux;
  • unused code removed.
