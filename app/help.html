How to use

  1. On the Convert tab, choose or drop your disc files. Pick the .cue (or .gdi, CloneCD .ccd, Alcohol 120% .mds/.mdf, DiscJuggler .cdi, Nero .nrg, GOG .ins, or cdrdao .toc) together with all of its track files (including .flac, .wav, .aiff, .ogg, or .mp3 audio tracks), or drop .zip / .7z archives directly.
  2. Each game appears as a card. The right chdman command is chosen automatically; open Options to change compression, hunk size or the output name.
  3. Press Create CHD on a card, or Start all at the bottom. Jobs run one after another.
  4. When a job finishes, press Download. On desktop Chrome and Edge you can also Save all to folder.
  5. On iPhone and iPad the button is called Save to Files and opens the share sheet, which works even inside apps that can't download. Results over 512 MB in Safari or viewer apps (256 MB in a Home Screen web app, where iOS enforces a tighter memory cap), such as most DVD games, are downloaded instead, since the share sheet loads the whole file into memory. Safari puts them in Files → Downloads; apps like Sitecase usually can't, so for big games use Discpress in Safari at powerbeef.github.io/discpress/.

Add .chd files to extract them back to .cue/.bin, .gdi, a cdrdao .toc, .iso, a hard disk .img, a LaserDisc .avi or a raw image, to verify their checksums, or to see their details. A CD comes back as a Redump dump by default: the same cue sheet and one .bin per track, so the files match Redump's checksums. With Settings → Keep the cue sheet in CD CHDs, new CHDs also keep the original cue sheet, so what CHDs don't otherwise hold (CATALOG, FLAGS, ISRC, extra indexes, CD-i track types) comes back too. Saturn, Sega CD and PC Engine CD games leave it out: Kronos, Yabause and jgenesis can't open a CHD that keeps one.

A lone .bin without its .cue still works for single-track data discs (a cue is generated), but for discs with music tracks always add the original .cue.

ECM images (.bin.ecm, packed with the ecm tools) can take the place of their .bin: add them with the .cue. They are unpacked as they are read, and a damaged one is reported.

A .zip or .7z archive works like the files inside it: add the archive of a game (or several), and its cue sheet finds its tracks inside. Compressed files stream straight into the browser's private storage and are checked against their CRC-32 checksums (password-encrypted archives and .rar files must be unpacked externally first).

A PlayStation EBOOT.PBP (made with popstation for a PSP or PS Vita) converts to a CD CHD: each disc in it gets its own card, named after the game in the file. PlayStation Store downloads are encrypted and can't be read.

Alcohol 120% v1.x (.mds + .mdf) and DiscJuggler (.cdi v2/v3/v3.5) images are read directly: single-session discs and DVD .mds/.mdf images convert straight to CHD (and PS1 .mdf interleaved subchannels auto-extract LibCrypt .sbi files). Multi-session .cdi images identify the game, recommend keeping the .cdi for Flycast/Redream, and offer 1-click extraction to .cue + .bin (plus an optional Flycast 2.8+ Tier X CHD mode for 2-session Audio+Data Dreamcast MIL-CDs). Daemon Tools v2 .mdx and UltraISO .isz must be converted first.

Cue sheets referencing audio tracks (.flac, extended/24-bit/RF64 .wav, .aiff/.aifc, .ogg, .mp3, and GOG .ins) decode on demand into 44.1 kHz 16-bit stereo Red Book PCM: lossless tracks match Redump/DAT checksums byte-for-byte, while lossy tracks display a warning badge. CD+G cues (TRACK nn CDG) and CloneCD .ccd + .sub (opt-in Tier X toggle in Options) store RW_RAW subchannels with warnings for emulators that reject them (Beetle PCE/Saturn/PC-FX and Flycast ≤ 2.7).

Use the Target selector at the top of the page (Universal, Fast Universal, ES-DE / PC, Handheld, MiSTer FPGA, or Legacy) to match your emulator setup, including reversible Tier X cue/disc layout repairs (stored with a DPRC0001 trailer so Extract restores the original cue) and automatic .m3u folder layouts. Dropping multiple existing .chd files opens the CHD Health Audit banner, checking 12 common compatibility bugs with 1-click lossless fixes. Original Xbox .iso dumps default to creating a scrubbed .xiso.iso for xemu, and PSP/PS2 DVDs (or DVD CHDs) can also output --block=2048 CSO v1, CSO v2, or ZSO for real hardware and OPL.

The type and hunk size are set from the console once the game is identified. With the default Universal target, compression stays chdman's default, which every emulator reads (DOSBox Pure, for PC discs, opens .zip files directly or uncompressed CD CHDs, and the card says so); the page warns when the Zstd or MiSTer preset isn't readable by a console's emulators (Saturn, 3DO, Sega CD, PC Engine CD, Amiga, PlayStation, PS2, Dreamcast, NAOMI, PC-FX). NAOMI, NAOMI 2, Triforce and Chihiro GD-ROMs are named after their disc serial (such as gds-0014.chd), which MAME and Flycast look for in the game's romset folder. GameCube, Wii, Xbox 360 and PS3 emulators don't load CHDs at all (Original Xbox defaults to scrubbed .xiso.iso for xemu instead, and Analogue Pocket openFPGA cores require .cue/.bin, which you can get back with Extract), and the page says so. Neo Geo CD CHDs work in NeoCD, FBNeo, MAME and MiSTer. It also says when a common emulator can't load a CHD of the disc: PCSX2 plays only the first track of a PS2 CD with music tracks, Flycast 2.7 and earlier can't load CD-based Dreamcast discs whose tracks have pregaps, Flycast can’t load Dreamcast or NAOMI CHDs with MODE2/2048, MODE2/2324 or MODE2_FORM_MIX tracks, a disc with more than one session is read from the wrong place, Jaguar CD emulators may not load a Jaguar CD CHD at all (MiSTer's Jaguar core loads .cdi only), a 2,048-byte PlayStation or CD-i ISO has lost data, PicoDrive doesn’t load Sega CD images of 80 minutes or more, Sega CD 32X games need PicoDrive, jgenesis or ares, and some Saturn, Sega CD, PC Engine, PC-FX, PlayStation and Neo Geo CD emulators misread particular cue sheets. A PAL PlayStation game protected by LibCrypt warns when its .sbi file wasn't added with it. For MiSTer FPGA, choose the For MiSTer FPGA compression preset (Zstd and FLAC with small hunks, which MiSTer reads fast enough); the page says when MiSTer has no core for the console.

Add a PAL PlayStation game's .sbi file (LibCrypt protection data), or DuckStation's .lsd, with its .cue and .bin: it is paired by filename, identified title, or PS1 serial and saved next to the CHD, under the CHD's name, where emulators look for it. A CloneCD, DiscImageCreator or redumper dump's .sub or .subcode holds the same data: for a PlayStation game, the page reads it and saves the .sbi for you. When two or more discs of a game are converted (for systems whose emulators use playlists: PS1, Saturn, Sega CD, Dreamcast, PCE CD, PC-FX, CD32, CDTV), the first disc's card offers an .m3u playlist: keep it with the CHDs and open it, so the emulator can swap discs. For PS2 and PSP multi-disc games, open Disc 1 directly and switch discs in the emulator menu instead (PCSX2 and PPSSPP do not open .m3u playlists). Until all the discs of a set are converted, a finished disc says how many the set has.

Game identification and renaming

Every disc you add is examined to find its console (PlayStation 1/2/PSP, Saturn, Sega CD, Dreamcast, PC Engine CD, PC-FX, Neo Geo CD, 3DO, CD-i, Amiga CD32/CDTV, Jaguar CD, NAOMI, GameCube, Wii, Xbox, PS3 and more) and its serial number, read from the disc's own boot files and headers. Video CDs, UMD Video and other video discs (DVD-Video, Blu-ray, HD DVD, DVD-Audio, Photo CD) are recognized as films, with a note that video players don't open CHDs.

The game is then looked up in a built-in copy of the Redump database (over 33,000 discs, via libretro-database, /*DBVER*/). When a track's size and CRC-32 checksum match, the dump is an exact, verified copy; otherwise the serial number is used, along with the disc's size and, on Sega's consoles, which disc of a set it is and the regions it was sold for. Results are named after the official title, for example Metal Gear Solid (USA) (Disc 1) (Rev 1).chd. You can turn this off in Settings or type your own name in Options.

Each finished CHD has a Verify button: it reads the CHD back and checks it holds exactly the data it was made from. Settings → Verify each new CHD after creating it does it every time, and it is done by itself when a helper thread fails during a conversion.

For existing .chd files, choose Rename to save a copy with the official name. Verify checks the CHD's own checksums, then compares it with Redump without extracting it: the file the built-in database lists for the disc (its ISO, its only .bin, or its main data track) must have Redump's size and CRC-32.

To check every track, add Redump's or No-Intro's DAT files for your consoles (the .dat or .xml, or its .zip), or add them in Settings → Your DAT files. They are kept in the browser. Each disc is then checked against them before converting, and each CHD after Verify: when every track matches a game in a DAT, the card says it is verified and the result is named after that game.

Good to know

Privacy

Nothing is uploaded. The real chdman from MAME runs inside your browser as WebAssembly; files are read directly from your device.

Big files

Results are written to the browser's private storage on disk (not to memory), so multi-gigabyte DVD images work if your device has the free space. During CHD creation in private storage, progress is checkpointed periodically to disk so interrupted jobs can resume after a reload or mobile tab kill instead of starting over. Some browsers give no such storage to a page opened as a file (Chrome and Edge among them): the page then keeps results in memory and says so at the top, and after a reload it lists the results that were lost. For large DVD images there, choose a folder, or use Discpress online. Download results when they are ready; the storage is cleared when you use Free temporary storage or reopen the page, except unsaved results and resumable checkpoints, which the page lists under From your last visit. On desktop Chrome and Edge you can instead have results written straight into a folder of your choice (Settings → Where to keep results).

Speed

Creating a CHD is CPU-heavy. This page spreads compression, and extracting and verifying too, over several CPU threads (see Settings). Expect a few minutes for a CD on a computer and longer on a phone. Nearly as small, faster in Options tries each track with only the codec that suits it: about 1.7 times as fast for CDs and 1.5 for DVDs, with files at most 0.3% bigger. Faster to create trades more size for more speed.

On phones and tablets

The layout adapts to your screen, text size, notch, rotation and keyboard. In a normal browser the whole page scrolls; inside apps that preview files (and when installed on the home screen) the header stays fixed and only the content scrolls. If scrolling ever feels wrong, change it in Settings → Troubleshooting → Scrolling; Screen info, in the same place, shows what your device reports.

Keep this page open and in the foreground while it works; the screen is kept awake automatically. On iPhone and iPad, iOS pauses a page within seconds of locking the phone or switching apps, and may reload it to free memory. Finished results not yet saved — and checkpoints of interrupted CHD conversions when private storage is available — are kept under From your last visit the next time the page opens; re-adding the original files lets you press Resume CHD to finish from the last checkpoint.

Two ways to run it. Discpress is one HTML file you can download and keep, or use online at powerbeef.github.io/discpress/ with nothing to download. It's the same file, published with each release, and it works on your device in both cases: the page may not connect anywhere. Once opened, the online version works offline too.

iPhone and iPad: use Discpress online, in Safari. Safari can't open a downloaded HTML file, and apps that can, such as Sitecase, usually can't save results over 512 MB. In Safari, results of any size download to Files → Downloads. Share → Add to Home Screen keeps it one tap away; if a big download doesn't start from there, open the page in Safari itself.

Compatibility of the results

The CHDs are standard CHD v5 files made by chdman from the official MAME 0.289 release, with the same checksums as desktop chdman 0.289 and the very same bytes as chdman 0.289 on Linux (unless you choose Smaller still, Nearly as small, faster, or Faster to create, or keep cue sheets in CD CHDs, which change the compressed bytes or non-checksummed metadata but not the checksums), so they work in MAME, RetroArch, DuckStation, PCSX2, PPSSPP, Flycast and other emulators. The Zstandard and For MiSTer FPGA options need an emulator that supports Zstandard, and opt-in Tier X layout repairs (--sessions, --trailer) alter disc layout or metadata while preserving lossless restoration via Extract. For older emulators, turn on Settings → Compatibility mode for older emulators (or Target → Legacy): PS2 games on DVD then become CD CHDs, which AetherSX2, NetherSX2 and PCSX2 builds from before April 2023 need (DVD CHDs came with chdman 0.255), and the Zstandard presets warn for every console.

Everything else

The Advanced tab exposes every chdman command and option: copy, createraw, parent CHDs, hard disk templates, addmeta, delmeta, dumpmeta, listtemplates and more.

About and licenses

Discpress /*VERSION*/ contains chdman from MAME 0.289 (the official release, tag mame0289), compiled to WebAssembly with Emscripten. Its source (the parts of MAME chdman uses and external compression/math libraries in engine/, plus browser decoders and glue in wasm/) is kept in the Discpress repository. The changes:

  • a file-system bridge for reading your files and writing results;
  • helper workers that compress, and decompress for extracting and verifying, on several cores;
  • faster codec trials, hashing and CD error-correction codes that give the same bytes;
  • Redump's layout for extracted CDs (extractcd --redump), and an option to keep a disc's cue sheet in its CHD for that (createcd --keepcue);
  • a codec plan that tries each hunk only with the codecs meant for what it holds (--codecplan), and deflate by libdeflate (--libdeflate), for the faster presets;
  • reading CSO/ZSO compressed ISOs, ECM images, PS1 PBP files, Alcohol 120% .mds/.mdf, DiscJuggler .cdi, audio track cues (FLAC, extended WAV/RF64, AIFF, Ogg Vorbis, MP3), and .zip/.7z archives directly;
  • writing --block=2048 CSO v1/v2 and ZSO files for real PSP, PS Vita Adrenaline, and PS2 OPL hardware, plus scrubbed Xbox XISO (.xiso.iso) images for xemu;
  • crash-resumable OPFS checkpoints, reversible Tier X layout repairs (--sessions, --trailer, --stripmeta), and a 12-check CHD Library Health Auditor;
  • fixes for chdman 0.289's crashes and silent failures on bad input;
  • the same floating-point math for the FLAC encoder in every build, so CD audio comes out byte for byte as with chdman on Linux;
  • unused code removed.
